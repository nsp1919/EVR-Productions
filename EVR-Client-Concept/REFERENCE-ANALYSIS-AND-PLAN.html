Use a light, spacious EVR identity with distinct wedding and corporate accents. Do not merge every feature or service from both companies.

2. The Big Day: detailed design and journey analysis

Visual system

  • Light, near-white canvas with soft warm/pink colour around the hero; ample whitespace rather than an all-dark film-studio treatment.
  • Floating rounded navigation, compact brand presence, short menu and a high-contrast red/pink message action.
  • Editorial serif headings with a clean sans-serif body. Rendered styles identify Cormorant-family headings and Plus Jakarta Sans navigation.
  • Hero pairs an emotional proposition with layered portrait film cards, titles, duration indicators and sound controls. The media demonstrates the style instead of merely decorating the page.
  • Rounded panels, restrained borders and soft shadows make the detailed content approachable.

Information architecture and conversion

  1. Emotional wedding introduction and immediate conversation CTA.
  2. Budget/date qualification before a lengthy sales conversation. The site publishes its own investment range; those figures do not apply to EVR.
  3. Complete wedding stories rather than only isolated highlights. The wedding library offers a five-question matching experience, then event/tradition browsing.
  4. Written delivery/process promises, named responsibilities and a detailed journey build trust before the final CTA.
  5. Team, client stories, FAQs and repeated contact/quote entry points address uncertainty.

Quotation experience

The public builder page first shows an example itemised quotation, explains price drivers such as events, crew, albums and extras, then introduces a three-step journey. The initial step asks for names, WhatsApp, wedding date and city. The page describes minimum coverage rules and optional additions.

What this changes for EVR: a public quotation journey is an important reference signal. The client has not explicitly selected staff-only pricing. Show a customer estimate path in the concept and make its final production scope a decision, rather than silently removing it.

Adapt, do not copy

Use the principles of media-first storytelling, relevant wedding galleries, process clarity and explainable estimates. Do not copy their exact typography combination, headline, card artwork, family photographs, team details, customer app, OTP workflow, mandatory crew rules, delivery penalties, testimonials, price range or scale claims. EVR must supply its own operating commitments.

3. GCS: detailed design and journey analysis

Visual system

  • Bright neutral first screen, cool blue highlights, bold sans-serif headlines and rounded pill-shaped actions.
  • The mobile hero centres its proposition and stacks the main actions; a compact “More” menu reduces header density.
  • The full homepage uses contrasting sections, larger type, motion/video-led work and a dedicated short-form content area.
  • The rendered navigation and headings use Manrope-family sans-serif styling. EVR can follow the clarity and hierarchy without reproducing the identity.

Information architecture and conversion

  1. Broad agency proposition with primary call-booking and secondary service discovery.
  2. Service overview spanning creative and technology disciplines.
  3. Approach/ethos followed by short-form reels and selected client work.
  4. A separate technology capability section, then testimonials, client names/logos and business metrics.
  5. Repeated project/contact actions and detailed footer navigation.

Relevant inner pages

The content-production page specifies planning, reels/campaigns/brand films, scripts, direction and editing, with a direct project action. The work index is organised around named clients with selectable project entries. The contact form qualifies name, brand, email, phone, industry, service, budget, timeline and project detail, and includes a processing-consent checkbox.

Adapt, do not copy

EVR should use the service clarity, reel-first portfolio treatment, project summaries and structured business brief. Do not advertise GCS's website development, AI agents, CRM, SEO, paid marketing or application services under EVR without confirmation. Do not transfer client logos, engagement claims, ROAS or campaign statistics. Budget/timeline choices should reflect EVR's business, not copied agency thresholds.

CUSTOMER-FACING CONCEPT

Choose wedding or corporate → adjust shoot days/output requirements → view an indicative breakdown → request final confirmation. The prototype uses sample rates and avoids a lead gate to keep the presentation safe and simple.

STAFF WORKFLOW

Prepare the final quotation

Confirm the brief → select services → edit rates and quantities → apply agreed charges → save the issued rate snapshot → preview/export the PDF. Production authentication and database storage are not present in the demo.

  2. 20–49%: expansion. Six original illustrated media placeholders spread around an EVR-specific statement.

  6. 83–95%: choose your world. Wedding and corporate panels arrive; their links become interactive when visible.
  7. End: release. The stage unpins into the regular homepage. Reverse scrolling retraces every phase and preserves the selected front card.

The V4 desktop sequence adds 330 small-viewport-height units of scroll distance; mobile adds 260. A permanent Skip intro control exits to the main services. The animation reads native scroll position through requestAnimationFrame; it does not intercept wheel/touch input or impose a scrolling speed. There is no perpetual frame loop.

Accessibility and fallbacks: reduced-motion mode removes the scroll runway and shows the initial static hero; no JavaScript also leaves the static hero usable. Invisible links are inert, the skip control moves keyboard focus to services, and changing the motion preference live restores a usable layout. Below the hero, existing short reveal/hover effects remain.

All visuals are original illustration placeholders. Actual EVR films or photographs are still required to achieve the photographic finish of the references. The motion preview is a real local browser recording. Measurements are from this test viewport and session, not fixed claims about every device or future version of the reference sites.

The scene is calculated from native scroll position and reverses with upward scrolling. Four reusable chapter scenes provide scroll-synchronised artwork, chapter highlighting and progress. Desktop artwork moves vertically; mobile artwork moves horizontally so it cannot pass behind the reading copy. Reverse scrolling restores the sequence.
